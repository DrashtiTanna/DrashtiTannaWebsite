Research question

“What are the values of the landscape that have been lost in the past, that once replaced could rebalance the interactions of the infrastructure in the territory?”

This question shaped the way we looked at the site — not only through its physical structures, but through the hidden values embedded in water systems, agricultural patterns and everyday perceptions.

01 — Territory: an historical review

A plain cut by infrastructure

The construction of the BreBeMi motorway and the railway fragmented large portions of the land, interrupting the historical continuity of fields, canals and settlements between the Adda, Serio and Oglio rivers.

Comparing the features of the landscape in 1975 and 2012 shows the loss: strips of forest located peripherally have disappeared over time as a result of the agriculture industrialization process. Yet key structuring elements survive — the fontanili, the productive fields, the irrigation canals, the historic Fosso Bergamasco — elements that once defined the identity of the plain.

The site sits within a larger regional system: the national bike lane AIDA and the landscape guide trails position it between infrastructure and heritage.

02 — Layers of preexistent values

The fontanili: springs of the plain

Around the Serio River, the geological line of the springs marks a geomorphological phenomenon linked to impermeable shallow stratification. The fontanili are a peculiar element of this land — fresh-water springs of a distinctive semi-natural origin, shaped over centuries by human intervention.

They provided fresh water for irrigation and created semi-natural flooded landscapes in winter. Mapping the active, inactive and extinct fontanili revealed a green connectivity network waiting to be reactivated according to their footprint.

Recognizing these elements helped us understand what had been lost — and what could be brought back. From the project statement

03 — The field of intervention

Infraspaces:
more than empty margins.

From the railway, the landscape reveals itself as a sequence of fragmented boundaries and residual spaces, where canals, fields, settlements and infrastructures meet without clarity.

These infraspaces and diffuse borders were studied carefully — some squeezed between canals and productive fields, others beside settlements or underneath raised roads, others stretched along the railway. Using graphic codes and annotations, their boundaries were defined and they were recognized as potential active components of a new landscape strategy.

A mapping process identified all the elements contributing to border definition in the area of intervention: tunnels, points of decision, landmarks, tree-lined strips, canals and the sections that cut across them.

04 — Water as the central theme

Collect, filter, reinfiltrate

Drainage from the BreBeMi and the railway currently flows without management — often polluted and disconnected from the natural hydrology. The proposal introduces widened drainage canals, bioswales and detention ponds to collect, filter and reintegrate these flows.

A

Identify the infraspaces

Identification of the existing condition of infra-spaces and their interaction with blue, green and gray infrastructures — sustainable water drainage, biodiversity and water resilience.

B

Reforest the nodes

At node conditions between blue and gray infrastructures: a reforestation plan including shrubs, bushes and tree species, connecting the existing forest patches.

C

Let water flow

Flow of water as a preexistence — promote water filtration with polyspecific herbaceous and tree species, and widen the water crossings between the infrastructures.

D

Clean the drainage

BreBeMi drainage water pollution: a network of inlet and outlet bioswale canals with filtration purpose — collect and reinfiltrate the water in a detention pond.

Transects of the network 1:500

Forestation crossings (A–A′) — between the BreBeMi motorway and the railway, layered vegetation creates a biodiverse space where a linear passage gives access and connection to the Serio River.

Collect & reinfiltrate the water (B–B′) — gray water from both infrastructures is filtered through canals and a detention pond, which connects to the wider water system. A separate pond naturalizes water from the irrigation canals linked to the fontanili.

Recreational infraspaces (C–C′) — openings in the embankments filter gray water through the landscape, while reforestation blends ecological restoration with recreational spaces in the corridor.

05 — Vegetation typology

Native species, by function

Planting typologies use native species by function — layered forests, green barriers, bioswales and detention ponds — to enhance ecological resilience, manage water and strengthen landscape connectivity.

Forestation area

  • Platanus × acerifolia
  • Platanus orientalis
  • Fagus sylvatica
  • Quercus cerris · Quercus robur
  • Populus nigra · Populus alba
  • Salix alba

Tall layered forest along the crossings — shade, biomass, biodiversity.

Green barrier

  • Quercus ilex
  • Acer pseudoplatanus · Acer campestre
  • Tilia platyphyllos
  • Ostrya carpinifolia · Fraxinus ornus
  • Carpinus betulus · Betula pendula

Dense screens against noise and views of the gray infrastructure.

Detention pond

  • Corylus avellana
  • Rhamnus cathartica
  • Malus sylvestris
  • Prunus mahaleb
  • Arbutus unedo · Sorbus domestica

Soft banks that store, clean and slowly release the drainage water.

Bioswale

  • Phillyrea angustifolia
  • Amelanchier ovalis
  • Viburnum tinus · Viburnum lantana
  • Crataegus monogyna

Filtering strips that carry water from the infrastructure to the ponds.
